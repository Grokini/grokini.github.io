Foundation

Why weekly RSI matters

The Relative Strength Index has measured momentum exhaustion since J. Welles Wilder introduced it in 1978. The weekly timeframe specifically filters out intraday noise and identifies setups where selling pressure (or buying pressure) has become statistically extreme — the conditions historically associated with mean reversion.

But weekly RSI alone isn't enough. Single-timeframe extremes produce too many false signals. Alpha RSI's edge is not RSI itself — it's the disciplined engineering around it: multi-timeframe confirmation, convergence gating, tier progression, SPY-relative decoupling, and exit discipline. These layers are what turn a 50-year-old indicator into a system worth paying attention to.

PILLAR 1
Multi-timeframe confirmation
Weekly, daily, and monthly RSI must agree. Single-timeframe extremes are noisy. Alignment across timeframes is what separates real exhaustion from temporary retracement.
PILLAR 2
Tier-based maturation
A single scan of a single setup tells you little. Setups that hold through multiple scans and confirm across five signals — price basing, gap tightening, IV stability, RSI slope flattening, daily slope turning — are statistically much stronger.
PILLAR 3
Exit discipline equals entry discipline
For options traders, wrong exits destroy right entries. The four-signal exit system — protecting from broken thesis, locking in mid-move profits, flagging time decay — treats exiting as a first-class feature, not an afterthought.
Alpha RSI does not present historical backtests. The evidence of the system's performance is the live paper trade dashboard — every trade the scanner has executed since launch, publicly visible. Performance data will accumulate with every scan. Not financial advice.

FAQ

Common questions

What is RSI and why does it matter for options? +
RSI (Relative Strength Index) measures whether a stock is overbought or oversold based on recent price movements. Extreme RSI readings — especially on the weekly chart — historically precede significant price reversals, which is exactly what options traders want to position ahead of.
How many alerts will I get per day? +
Alert volume varies dramatically with market conditions. Quiet markets might produce 0-3 alerts per day. Volatile markets can produce 15+ alerts as more stocks hit statistical extremes. Each stock is capped at one new alert per day so you never get spammed with the same ticker — subsequent tier advancements and exit signals on that ticker still notify you as conditions change.
Do I need trading experience to use these alerts? +
Basic options knowledge is helpful — understanding calls, puts, and risk sizing will help you act on alerts effectively. Every alert discloses its full scoring basis (which factors contributed to the score) so you can evaluate the setup on its merits. The public paper trade dashboard also shows exactly what the scanner does with every Perfect Setup alert, so you can see the mechanics in practice before committing real capital.
How do I receive the alerts? +
All alerts are delivered through Telegram — a free messaging app available on iPhone, Android, and desktop. Once you subscribe you'll be added to the private Alpha RSI channel where alerts appear in real time.
Is this financial advice? +
No. Alpha RSI is an educational tool that provides data-driven signals for informational purposes only. Nothing we send constitutes financial advice. Always conduct your own research and consult a licensed financial advisor before making trading decisions.
Can I cancel anytime? +
Yes, absolutely. Cancel anytime directly through Whop with no questions asked. Your access continues until the end of your billing period.
How is this different from TradingView alerts? +
TradingView alerts require you to set up each ticker manually and monitor it yourself. Alpha RSI scans approximately 12,000 NYSE/NASDAQ equities automatically every 30 minutes, scores each qualifying signal across 10+ factors, applies convergence and sync gating, tracks setups through a four-tier maturity system, and delivers complete briefs — full factor disclosure, multi-timeframe RSI readings, 52-week context, and chart links — straight to your Telegram. And every Perfect Setup is then executed in a live paper account you can watch publicly.
Why is the subscriber count capped at 200? +
Signal quality can degrade when too many traders act on the same small-cap alert simultaneously — spreads widen, premiums spike, and the edge evaporates into noise. The 200-seat cap is a signal-preservation decision, not a marketing tactic. Once filled, a waitlist opens. Current subscribers keep their seat as long as they remain subscribed.
How do I verify the paper trade account is legit? +
The public dashboard at grokini.github.io/paper-trades.html displays live Alpaca paper account data: current equity, open positions with real-time P&L, every closed trade with entry/exit/reason/P&L, and performance statistics. The page regenerates after every scan. You can watch the scanner's own paper account rise or fall before you subscribe, while subscribed, or after canceling — the page is always public.
What account size do I need? +
Alerts span a wide range of underlying prices — from single-digit stocks where contracts run under $100, to large-caps where contracts can run into the thousands. Every alert shows the current option price so you can size the position appropriately for your account. Responsible position sizing is your decision; we don't dictate it.
